Implied Price Calculator

model

Pick a common to get an implied share price, or a preferred to see whether its dividends stay funded — and at what treasury price they wouldn't.

How the model works
  • Month-by-month engine — issuance buys the treasury asset (BTC, or SOL for DFDV), dividends, debt interest and operating costs get serviced, and for a common each month's price backs out of your mNAV.
  • Starts from today's live balance sheet. DFDV's SOL loans are SOL owed, marked at each month's SOL price.
  • Defaults are real — issuance uses each company's rolling 3-month pace from its filings (MSTR preferred sits at $0 while the STRC ATM is paused below par). A common's ending mNAV starts at its multiple today, so nothing re-rates unless you set it; a preferred starts with BTC or SOL going nowhere. Override anything.
  • Dividend cash runway — issuance proceeds first park N months of preferred dividends as cash (MSTR's recent posture); only the rest buys the treasury asset. Parked cash never earns the mNAV multiple, so it's per-share neutral and a longer runway means less accretion. 0 = every dollar buys the asset.
  • Issuance is earned, not assumed — the common ATM shuts in any month the modeled net mNAV sits below your floor (default 1.00×, Strategy's stated accretion threshold). Dividends, interest and costs then follow a waterfall: today's cash first, then runway cash, then issuance only while the ATM is open, then forced sales of the treasury. That's what makes a premium collapse actually hurt — you lose the accretion engine and start selling coin at the same moment.
  • Digital Credit — STRC, SATA and CHAD run their issuer's balance sheet from the preferred holder's seat. No new money comes in unless you add it. Opening cash pays first, then sales of the treasury. Debt interest and any convertible due come before everything else: one out of the money today is repaid in cash at its first put or maturity, with no refinancing. Operating costs come next, then every preferred series at its own rate. Whatever can't be paid is a modeled funding gap, not a legal default.
  • When can't it pay? — the break-even is the lowest ending treasury price that still funds every payment through your horizon, holding everything else. Raising preferred below its stated amount adds more claim than cash: $1M at $8 is 125,000 CHAD shares and $1.25M of stated notional.
  • Crash test — dips the treasury price that far below the straight line, bottoming in the month you pick, then back to your target by the horizon. Same ending price, different path: coin sold near the bottom to pay the bills is gone for good, so a crash can open a gap the straight line never shows. The break-even keeps your crash and moves only the ending price.

EV basis prices the whole structure (mcap = mNAV × crypto NAV − claims); net basis prices the common's residual (mcap = mNAV × net NAV). Converts are held at notional throughout — the model doesn't flip in-the-money tranches into share count. Runway cash offsets claims at face value but never earns the multiple — only asset-bought dollars capture the issuance premium. Today's cash pays first, then runway cash, issuance and sales; spending it adds to claims.
